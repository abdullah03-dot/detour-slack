**Discrete-event check.** We re-evaluated the regime calls in a discrete-event simulation built on the SeQUeNCe kernel [wu2021sequence] with full density matrices: purification and swapping are gate circuits (bilateral CNOTs, Bell-state measurement, Pauli corrections) with outcomes sampled by the Born rule, memory noise is a Kraus channel applied for the time each pair is actually stored, and fidelity is read from the density matrix. With the paper's assumptions (Mode F: twirled BBPSSW, instantaneous purification, storage charged to the delivered pair) the simulation reproduces every clean-path fidelity to numerical precision and all 12 regime calls of Table~\ref{tab:des}, including the 5$\times$5, $r=3$ call with margin $\delta=0.0039$; this checks the closed-form algebra and its implementation, not the model. The physical test is Mode R, which resolves the storage time of every stored pair.

The regime map is specific to the purification protocol. With DEJMPS in place of twirled BBPSSW (ideal gates), $F^*(0)$ on 5$\times$5, $r=3$ is 0.934 instead of 0.831 and the slack is at least 8; 6$\times$6, $r=3$, infeasible under BBPSSW, has slack at least 8. With gate error $\epsilon=0.01$ the 5$\times$5, $r=3$ slack is $-1$ under BBPSSW and 4 under DEJMPS (Table~\ref{tab:desgate}). Proposition~1 needs only monotonicity and is unaffected, but the closed form (\ref{eq:profile}) and the regime map must be recomputed for each protocol, and DEJMPS slacks exceed the range $s\le5$ in which Dijkstra was observed to match the oracle. Omitting the twirl (bilateral CNOT only) leaves phase errors uncorrected; no configuration is feasible.

In Mode R (Poisson raw-pair generation, nested recurrence on each link, swapping as soon as both links are ready, classical messages at $2\times10^5$\,km/s, $\kappa$ kept) at the paper's coherence times: at $R=10^{5}$\,Hz, 12 of 12 calls agree with the closed form at the same $R$ and the delivered fidelity is lower by 0.0008--0.0025; at $R=10^{4}$\,Hz, 7 of 12 calls agree with the closed form at the same $R$ and the delivered fidelity is lower by 0.0051--0.0195 (changed: 5$\times$5, $r=3$ (half): $s=1\to0$; 5$\times$5, $r=4$ (deph2): $s=2\to1$; 5$\times$5, $r=4$ (depol2): $s=2\to1$; 5$\times$5, $r=4$ (half): $s=2\to1$; 6$\times$6, $r=4$ (half): $s=1\to0$). The closed form charges storage only to the delivered pair ($t=\kappa D+C_r/R$), whereas the simulation charges every stored pair for its own waiting time.

At slow generation ($R=100$\,Hz and 1\,kHz) the design chart is optimistic: the coherence time at which the simulation makes the 5$\times$5 request routing-sensitive is 1.8--3.2 times the closed-form value (Table~\ref{tab:desdesign}). For depolarizing memories it lies between the optimistic and the pessimistic accounting in all 8 cases, so the two closed forms bracket it.

Evaluating every router's path in the simulation on the paper's 5$\times$5, $r=3$ trials (same seeds, Mode F) reproduces the success of every router on every trial (BFS 73.16\%, PG 96.62\%, oracle 98.24\%).

With timing resolved (1\,kHz, half, deph2, depol2 memories), the routing headroom (paper-model oracle minus BFS) is +3.0, +2.1, +2.7\,pp at a $T_2$ where the simulation gives $s=0$ and +23.4, +23.8, +23.8\,pp where it gives $s=1$. At $s=0$ the headroom is significant (exact McNemar $P<0.05$) in 3 of 3 settings. In every such case the extra successes are detours whose mean fidelity is below threshold but which succeed in a fraction 0.122, 0.086, 0.108 of attempts, because storage times vary from delivery to delivery: near the boundary the regime call is soft, and Proposition~1 applies to each delivery's fidelity rather than to the mean. On clean links a one-hop detour changes the mean establishment time (without $\kappa D$) by +0.8\,ms (half); +0.8\,ms (deph2); +0.8\,ms (depol2). On clean links a one-hop detour changes the mean latency including $\kappa D$ by +2.0\,ms (half); +2.0\,ms (deph2); +1.9\,ms (depol2). Counting every attempt back to back, the delivery rate at $s=1$ is 34.8 pairs/s with the oracle's paths and 25.6 with BFS (half memory): routing raises the delivery rate, by about the ratio of the success probabilities.
